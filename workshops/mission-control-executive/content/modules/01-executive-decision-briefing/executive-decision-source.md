## Audience, example, and terms

The primary audience is the executive sponsor, investment authority,
policy/governance authority, and business-outcome owner. Engineering, platform,
architecture, risk, finance, and measurement leaders support and challenge the
evidence.

The running example asks whether to fund a bounded pilot that permits an
approved AI coding assistant to support defined drafting tasks in one named
team's pull-request-based production change-control workflow. It is a planning
example, not a product-capability or outcome claim.

- **Done and accepted:** work reaches the agreed done point and passes the
  required human, quality, security, risk, and compliance checks.
- **What would have happened without the pilot (comparison):** the best
  supported account of the result without the pilot change.
- **What we can reasonably credit to the pilot (credit limit):** only the part
  of an observed difference that the evidence supports.
- **Full pilot cost:** direct pilot cost plus the pilot's declared share of
  common cost and work deferred elsewhere, with each item counted once.

The fixed title order is decision-first. **Govern -> Improve -> Prove -> Decide**
is narrative and visual wayfinding, not an alternate sequence.

<a id="s06-design-a-fair-comparison"></a>

## S06 Design a Fair Comparison

**00:11-00:14, 3 minutes: 3 instruction, 0 discussion.**

Visible teaching copy:

> **What changes in the pilot:** the approved assistant may draft ___;
> accountable humans review, change, accept, or reject the work.
>
> **What would have happened without the pilot (comparison):** comparable
> eligible changes without AI assistance, using a matched group at the same time
> or an equal-length earlier period chosen before results are interpreted
>
> **Keep the comparison fair:** People/work in scope ___ · Earlier period ___ ·
> Pilot period ___ · Same done and accepted rules ___ · Task/size mix ___
>
> **What else could explain the result:** staffing · change mix/complexity ·
> seasonality · release freezes · incidents · other tool or process changes
>
> **What we can reasonably credit to the pilot:** only the supported difference
> after other explanations and data-quality limits are recorded
>
> Without a trustworthy comparison, the result is a clue only and cannot
> support scale.

Narrative waypoint: Improve toward Prove; define what changes and how it can be
tested fairly.

Output: the pilot change, what would have happened without it, comparison scope
and periods, other possible explanations, and the credit limit.

Governing source: approved Mission Control S12, S21-S26, and S35.

## S08 Separate Technical Results from Business Outcomes

**00:17-00:21, 4 minutes: 4 instruction, 0 discussion.**

Visible teaching copy:

> **Technical results**
>
> share of changes accepted · developer effort/human checks · time from request

> security findings · rollbacks · incidents · policy exceptions
>
> **Business outcomes**
>

## S09 Count the Full Pilot Cost

**00:21-00:24, 3 minutes: 3 instruction, 0 discussion.**

Visible teaching copy:

> **Full pilot cost**
>
> Licenses + usage + implementation + training + support + governance +
> work deferred elsewhere (opportunity cost)
>
> For every cost: **amount or range · fixed or variable · people/work in scope ·
> period · source · owner · rule for dividing shared cost ·
> fact/assumption/unknown**
>
> Implementation includes validation or change management only when those are
> not tracked elsewhere.
>
> **Count each cost once.** Put each direct or shared cost under one rule.
>
> **Cost per accepted outcome = full pilot cost / accepted outcomes**
>
> Do not turn a benefit into money without an approved source and method.

Narrative waypoint: Prove; expose every cost behind the decision.

Output: a full pilot-cost view with ownership, period, source, shared-cost
rule, uncertainty, and no duplicate cost.

Governing source: approved Mission Control S10, S16, S27-S29, and S36; MCE-D01

## S11 Choose Stop, Revise, Fund, or Scale

**00:26-00:28, 2 minutes: 2 instruction, 0 discussion.**

Visible teaching copy:

> **Gate record**
>
> Decision: **Stop · Revise · Fund · Scale**
>
> Threshold/evidence ___ · Owner ___ · Decision authority ___ · Checkpoint ___
>
> If unmet ___ · If met ___
>
> **How the decision can grow**
>
> - **Gate 0 — Ready to test:** scope + controls + comparison -> fund bounded pilot
> - **Gate 1 — Evidence:** accepted outcomes + fair comparison + full cost ->
>   stop, revise, or fund controlled expansion
> - **Gate 2 — Ready to scale:** repeated evidence + owners and controls ready
>   to operate + named resources and authority -> scale
