Planning Checklist

  • Confirm dates and event hoursReserve both days from 9:00 AM-4:00 PM, including setup and participant arrival time.
  • Confirm venue and capacityProvide team seating, presentation space, accessible facilities, and room for facilitators.
  • Confirm audio and videoValidate projection, microphones, adapters, screen sharing, and any approved recording plan.
  • Confirm powerProvide an outlet for every participant laptop plus safe extension and charging capacity.
  • Confirm GitHub.com accessParticipants should be able to access GitHub.com if needed.
  • Confirm meals and breaksPlan Day 1 lunch for 12:00 PM-12:45 PM and Day 2 lunch for 12:00 PM-1:00 PM, plus scheduled breaks.
  • Finalize participant countConfirm attendee names, roles, accessibility needs, in-person or remote status, and team capacity.
  • Confirm on-site contactsIdentify the event owner, facilities contact, IT/network contact, security desk, and escalation path.
  • Confirm visitor accessDocument registration, badges, arrival instructions, building hours, parking, and after-hours restrictions.
  • Confirm leadership involvementAssign an executive kickoff speaker and leaders for Day 2 readouts, recognition, and next-step sponsorship.
  • Send participant invitationInclude the agenda, venue or Teams link, prerequisites, setup deadline, contacts, and hack preparation instructions.
  • Define remote participationUse one persistent collaboration room per team and require in-room members to join digitally for shared access.
  • Define team formationChoose pre-formed or day-of teams, identify team leads, and balance engineering skills and business context.
  • Confirm hack projectsSelect developer-work projects with an achievable scope, an accessible GitHub.com repository, a clear problem statement, and measurable impact assumptions.
  • Define safety and data boundariesIdentify prohibited data, secrets, production systems, destructive actions, and required human approvals.
  • Confirm readout formatEach team presents the problem, scope, estimated dollar impact and assumptions, next actions, and a short demo.
  • Set judging and awardsIf used, publish criteria, judges, timing, prizes, and how technical evidence and business impact are evaluated.
  • Prepare facilitation supportAssign workshop facilitators, mission support, hack mentors, timekeepers, and readout coordinators.

Hack Project and Readout Guidance

Good project sources

  • Engineering-manager nominations, technical debt, backlog or sprint work, and developer-productivity improvements.
  • Projects should be relevant to real developer work, safe to modify, and small enough to show verified progress in the available hack time.
  • Teams should know the repository, success criteria, constraints, test path, and required reviewers before Day 2 begins.

Readout structure

  • Problem statement and why it matters.
  • Project scope and what the team completed.
  • Estimated impact in dollars, including assumptions and confidence limits.
  • Technical evidence: changes, tests, review findings, and remaining risk.
  • Next-step actions and accountable owner.
  • Short demo.

Large-group option: for audiences above roughly 100 participants, use parallel heats or track-based readouts and send finalists or track winners to the main-stage session.
